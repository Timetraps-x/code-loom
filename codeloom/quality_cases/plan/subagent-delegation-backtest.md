# Plan Quality Case: Host Child-Agent Delegation Backtest

## Purpose

This is a maintainer-only oracle for testing the Claude Host Plan projection together with the existing `plan-architect`, `plan-reviewer`, and bounded child-agent prompts. It does not introduce a production child-agent API, change a project artifact, or grant a child authority over design.

A child may be used only when one unconfirmed fact can change the selected abstract model, enforcing mechanism, or material current-project landing. It must return source-qualified evidence only. `plan-architect` decides whether that evidence applies and retains every architecture, Owner, and final Plan decision.

## Full-Evidence Suppression: Asynchronous Dispatch Evolution

Use the complete `async-integration-evolution.md` business case.

The full confirmed-facts packet already establishes the local transaction-outbox boundary, at-least-once broker behavior, random carrier request identity, unsafe callback overwrite, historical identifier gap, legacy response meaning, and missing correlation. The Architect must not delegate merely to rediscover those supplied facts. It should directly select a design that makes the business dispatch identity, callback authority, historical evolution, compatibility, and recovery semantics explicit.

A child invocation in this full-evidence form is a false positive. The fact that the exact broker API, SQL dialect, retry count, or class layout is still project-local does not justify broad research when those choices do not alter the selected semantics.

## Required Delegation: Evidence-Limited Asynchronous Dispatch Projection

Use the accepted signals from `async-integration-evolution.md`, but deliberately withhold the current behavior of `CarrierDispatchConsumer`, `POST /carrier/callback`, and historical shipment identifiers from the Architect's initial evidence packet. Retain only the shared transaction-outbox fact, at-least-once/reordering broker behavior, the legacy `submitted=true` response, and these named child sources:

- `delivery_outbox` transaction path;
- `CarrierDispatchConsumer` delivery and carrier-request behavior;
- `POST /carrier/callback` lookup and write behavior;
- historical shipment rows without identifiers;
- the legacy `submitted=true` response path.

The Architect must formulate exactly one factual question that can change a business dispatch identity, callback correlation, migration, or recovery decision. The child scope is limited to the sources needed to answer that question, not every source in the case.

The child may return only `question`, `observed facts`, `constraints or counterevidence`, `unknowns`, and `decision relevance`, with a locatable source for each factual claim. It must not choose a generic coordinator, claim exactly-once delivery, select an architecture, decide an Owner question, write a Plan, assign Tasks, ask the user, or decide readiness.

The Architect must use source-qualified evidence to settle or qualify the business dispatch identity, callback authority, migration, and recovery design. It must explicitly reject a scope-free or non-material suggestion such as “add a generic coordinator” or “make the broker exactly once”; child opinion is not design authority.

If the child identifies an investigable carrier-contract fact that blocks a crash-window guarantee, the Architect must state the exact required evidence and its design impact. It must not turn that factual investigation into an Owner question unless the remaining alternatives are confirmed business, public-contract, irreversible, external-consequence, compliance, or risk-acceptance directions that evidence cannot decide.

## Required Delegation With Insufficient Evidence: Shared Replenishment

Use `replenishment-shared-capability.md` as the business case.

The Architect may formulate exactly one factual question about supplier-contract evidence for cancellation after acknowledgement and before final acceptance. The child can inspect only named supplier contract, integration, or existing cancellation-path evidence.

If those sources do not establish the external cancellation meaning, the child must report `insufficient evidence`. The Architect must not convert that uncertainty into a chosen external cancellation design. It must preserve the already-designable shared capability and then either surface the one bounded Owner decision already defined by the accepted case or state the exact missing contract evidence. It must not turn unrelated state, deduplication, callback, migration, or recovery design into broad unknowns.

## Forbidden Delegation: Closed Local Presentation Correction

Use `closed-local-correction.md` as the business case.

The confirmed facts already close the owner-fact and presentation correction. The Architect must not delegate. It should produce the narrow label correction, preserve the API/query/export/storage/elevator-use-unit boundaries, and avoid invented schema, Job, middleware, migration, transaction, performance, or PlantUML work.

## Forbidden Delegation With Review Closure: Cross-Layer Review State

Use `cross-layer-state-transition.md` and its seeded bad candidate.

The confirmed facts already decide that server authorization, legal transition ownership, durable history, concurrent terminal control, and customer read projection are material. The Architect must not use research delegation to avoid those decisions.

`plan-reviewer` must receive the exact candidate text and identity, construct its independent minimum baseline, and return evidence-backed counterexamples. Architect revision may change only affected mechanisms and dependencies. A re-review is valid only for the exact revised candidate and its material change scope.

## Pass and Failure Interpretation

A pass requires correct delegate-or-do-not-delegate behavior, exactly one bounded question when delegation is required, source-qualified child evidence, Architect evidence disposition, and preservation of Architect/Reviewer authority boundaries. It does not require one specific architecture or exact wording.

A failure includes broad or multiple questions; full-evidence or simple-case delegation; source-free factual claims; scope expansion; child architecture, Owner, Task, artifact, user-question, or readiness authority; Architect silently adopting child opinion; converting an investigable fact gap into a hidden design decision or premature Owner question; or candidate identity/re-review scope mismatch.

A clean Reviewer result means only that no material counterexample survived. It is not stage approval.
